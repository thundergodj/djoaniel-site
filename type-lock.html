How small can Instrument Serif go before it stops being Instrument Serif?

Instrument Serif is a single-weight display face with high stroke contrast. Its thin strokes measure roughly 1/15 of the cap height — so the hairline's rendered width is a direct function of size, and below a threshold it falls under one device pixel and the renderer greys it into mush. Scroll the ladder on the machine you actually design on and judge it with your eyes; the verdict column is the arithmetic.

03 · The locked rule

Instrument Serif never renders below 24px. Ever.

24px is where the hairline clears 1.0 device pixels on a standard 1× display — which is what most of your audience is on, since a 1920×1080 Windows laptop at 125% scaling is the single most common viewport. On a 2× retina panel you could cheat down to about 18px, but the site cannot detect that reliably enough to bet type on it. So: one floor, no exceptions.

04 · Consequence

The homepage now enforces this automatically. Above 900px viewport height, card titles are Instrument Serif at 24–27px. Below it, they swap to Radio Canada 700 rather than shrinking the serif into the failure zone. That swap is the readability rule from the north star doing visible work: the frame yields to the content, and nothing on the page is set below the size at which it was designed to be read.
